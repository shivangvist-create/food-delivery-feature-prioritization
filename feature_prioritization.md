# Feature Prioritization

## My Approach

For this case study, I looked at some common features that a food delivery app could add. Since a product team cannot build every feature at the same time, I wanted to compare them and see which features could be taken up first.

I used the RICE framework for this comparison.

## RICE Framework

The formula I used is:

**RICE Score = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort**

I considered four things for each feature:

- **Reach:** How many users may use the feature
- **Impact:** How useful the feature could be
- **Confidence:** How confident I am in the estimates
- **Effort:** How much work may be needed to build it

## Example

For example, for Smart Reorder:

- Reach = 8
- Impact = 3
- Confidence = 0.8
- Effort = 3

So,

**RICE Score = (8 × 3 × 0.8) / 3 = 6.4**

I used the same approach to compare the other features.

## What I Would Check Before Building

The RICE score is useful for comparing features, but I would not use it as the only deciding factor.

Before actually building a feature, I would also look at:

- What users are asking for
- Whether the feature supports the business goal
- Development effort and technical limitations
- Whether another feature is dependent on it
- Results from any previous experiments

## Next Step

Once a feature is selected, I would test it with a small group of users before launching it to everyone.

An A/B test can be used for this, where one group sees the existing experience and another group sees the new feature.

## Note

The numbers used here are assumptions for this case study. They are only being used to understand how the prioritization process works and are not real company data.
